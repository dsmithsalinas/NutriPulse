3. Footing is not medical care

Footing is not a medical device, and nothing in it is medical advice. It doesn’t diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease or condition. It records what you eat and do, and gives general nutrition and wellness information based on that.

Footing never gives dosing guidance. It can remind you of a schedule you entered. It won’t tell you to start, stop, raise, lower, delay or skip a dose. Pausing or stopping GLP-1 tracking in Footing only changes what the app shows you; it isn’t advice about your treatment.

Always talk to your doctor. Decisions about medication, nutrition targets, supplements, exercise or any health condition belong with a clinician who knows your history. If Footing and your clinician disagree, follow your clinician.

Calorie targets, macro goals, protein floors and other numbers Footing calculates are estimates from general formulas. Analytics and personal experiments show patterns in your own data, not causes. None of it is a personal clinical recommendation.

In an emergency

Don’t use Footing. Call your local emergency number (911 in the US) or go to an emergency room. Footing isn’t monitored, and no one is watching your logs in real time.

Disordered eating

Calorie and weight tracking can be harmful for people with a history of eating disorders. Footing is designed not to shame you, and Pulse is instructed to step back and point toward professional support if a conversation moves that way, but it isn’t a safeguard, a screening tool or a substitute for treatment. If you’re in recovery or at risk, talk to a clinician before using an app like this one.

4. About Pulse, the AI coach

Pulse is an AI assistant. Its replies are generated by a large language model (currently from Anthropic) using your message and a snapshot of your Footing data. That means:

  • It only runs with your agreement. Footing asks before anything is sent for Pulse, and you can turn Pulse off in Profile at any time. Logging and the rest of Footing keep working.
  • It can be wrong. AI produces confident, plausible, incorrect answers. Check nutrition figures and claims before relying on them.
  • It can miss an allergy. Pulse is told the allergies and foods you’ve saved and tries to avoid them, but it can still suggest something unsafe. Always check ingredients and labels yourself.
  • It only saves what you approve. When Pulse offers to remember something about you, nothing is saved until you tap Save.
  • It isn’t a clinician, dietitian, therapist or pharmacist, and no one supervises it.
  • It isn’t private counsel. Your messages and their data context are processed by our AI provider, as described in the Privacy Policy.
  • It will change. We tune the model, instructions and the data it can see over time.

You’re responsible for what you do with anything Pulse tells you. Treat it as a well-informed assistant that has read your logs, not a professional opinion.
