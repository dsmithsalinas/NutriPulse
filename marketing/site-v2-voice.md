# Footing — Site Voice Guide

**Scope:** every word on footing's marketing site, App Store listing, and emails.
**Source of truth for voice:** `docs/pulse-persona.md`. That file defines Pulse. This one
translates Pulse into a site. If the two ever disagree, the persona bible wins.

---

## 1. The one-line rule

> **Write every line the way Pulse would say it: specific, warm, and never grading anyone.**

Pulse's personality *is* the brand voice — that's already stated in the persona bible §1.
This is unusual and it is an advantage. Most companies invent a "brand voice" that nobody in
the product actually speaks. Footing's coach already has a fully-specified voice, it's
deployed in production, and users will meet it on day one. A site written in any other
register would be a promise the app then breaks.

So the site isn't *describing* a coach. It's the same coach, doing the talking.

---

## 2. Why "Footing" makes sense

Worth writing down, because half the copy leans on it.

A footing is the concrete base poured beneath a floor so nothing above it sinks. On a GLP-1,
protein is that base: the shot does its part, and your protein floor is what keeps the result
standing once it has.

That gives the name three readings, and the site uses all three:

| Reading | Where it's used |
|---|---|
| **Structural** — the base that carries the load | §06 "Why Footing?", the product claim |
| **Personal** — "finding your footing," regaining stability | the emotional arc of the scroll |
| **Physical** — the ground under you | the dark→daylight background arc |

**The positioning line that follows from it:** every other app in this category is named
after the medication or the outcome. This one is named after **the work**. That sentence is
the single best thing the name gives us and it should survive every future rewrite.

**Where the name goes on the page:** §06, near the end — *after* the value is proven, not
before. A brand-story section up top is a riddle the visitor has no reason to solve. Placed
late, the metaphor arrives as a reward, and named things get remembered.

## 3. Tone — five traits, and what each prevents

Lifted from the persona bible §2 and mapped onto marketing copy. Hold all five at once.

| Trait | On the site it sounds like | The failure it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| **Attentive** | "142g of protein with dinner still ahead — on a shot day." | Generic wellness copy that could belong to any app |
| **Precise** | Real numbers, inline, always: "43g from your floor." | Vague benefit-speak ("reach your goals!") |
| **Warm-direct** | Names the problem, then the way out, in the same breath. | Honesty that makes people feel small — or kindness that hides the truth |
| **Steady** | Same register on the hero and the FAQ. No hype spikes. | Landing-page mania. Exclamation marks. Fake urgency |
| **Protective** | Frames everything as defending progress, never correcting failure. | Any whiff of "you've been doing it wrong" |

### The non-shaming law applies to marketing too

The persona bible §3 outranks everything, including conversion instinct:

1. Never make the reader feel guilty for being on the medication.
2. Never imply the medication alone does the work.

On the site this has one extra consequence worth stating plainly: **when we describe the
reader's past failure, the failure belongs to the tools.** That's the load-bearing line in
§01:

> "And you quit. Not because you lack discipline — because nothing about that day was worth
> coming back to."

A visitor who finishes §01 thinking *that wasn't my fault* is listening for the rest of the
page. A visitor who feels accused is gone.

## 4. Rhythm

Pulse's rhythm is **short, short, long** — two clipped clauses that land a fact, then a
longer one that gives it somewhere to go. It reads like someone who knows the answer and
isn't performing.

> Strong, and on the hard version of a good day — 142g of protein with dinner still ahead,
> two days after your shot when appetite is usually lowest.

Rules:

- **Vary sentence length deliberately.** Three medium sentences in a row is the sound of
  filler. Follow a long sentence with a four-word one.
- **Em dashes carry the turn.** Footing's copy pivots mid-sentence a lot — that's the
  consequence→path move compressed into one breath. Don't replace them with semicolons.
- **One idea per paragraph, two to four sentences.** If a paragraph needs a fourth idea, it
  needed to be a section.
- **End sections on the short sentence.** "This one is named after the work." "Every other
  app, every single day." The stinger is where the beat lands.
- **Read it aloud.** If you run out of breath, it isn't Pulse.

## 5. Words

### Words we use

**From the persona bible §5, unchanged:** shot (human register) · dose (data register) ·
protein floor · protect / protecting · close the gap · finish strong · pacing · in your
corner · showing up · your numbers.

**Site-specific additions:**

| Word | Why |
|---|---|
| **floor** | The whole differentiator. A minimum you clear, not a target you beat. |
| **the work** | The name's payoff line. Reserve it for §06. |
| **notices** | What Footing does that other apps don't. |
| **coached** / **graded** | The two poles of the entire pitch. |
| **already** | "It already read your day." Implies attention that predates the question. |
| **paying attention** | The plainest statement of the product. |

### Words we ban

| Banned | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| **journey** | Wellness-brand filler. Means nothing. | what you're doing / this |
| **transform**, **unlock**, **empower**, **elevate** | Pitch-deck verbs. Nobody speaks them. | the specific thing that happens |
| **effortless weight loss**, **easy** (about the loss) | Both are dishonest and both feed the "easy way out" shame. | *easy* only ever describes the logging |
| **cheat**, **guilt-free**, **indulge**, **naughty**, **clean eating** | Moralised food language. Direct violation of the non-shaming law. | just name the food |
| **crush it**, **beast mode**, **no excuses**, **grind** | Drill-sergeant register. Pulse is a cornerman, not a bootcamp. | — |
| **just**, **simply** (as in "simply log your meal") | Minimises effort the reader found genuinely hard. | delete the word |
| **revolutionary**, **game-changing**, **the future of** | Unearned. | say what it does |
| **users**, **consumers** | In user-facing copy, it's *you*. | you |
| **AI-powered** as a benefit | The AI isn't the benefit; being read is. | "it's read your whole day" |
| **Track Your Macros Today!** (Title Case, exclamation) | Reads as a system, not as someone who knows you. | sentence case, no bang |

### Mechanics

- **Sentence case everywhere** — headlines, buttons, nav, section labels. Per persona §4.
  (Small-caps eyebrows are a typographic treatment, not Title Case; the underlying string is
  still sentence case.)
- **Numbers are always concrete and always inline.** "43g from your floor," not "close to
  your goal." Tabular figures so they don't jitter.
- **Exclamation marks: essentially never.** The site currently has zero. Keep it that way.
- **Emoji: at most one on the entire page**, and only on an earned win. Currently one 🔥, in
  the streak demo. Never near the medical disclaimer or the ED-adjacent copy.
- **Second person, present tense, contractions.** "You're 43g short," never "users have not
  met their target."
- **No markdown headers inside Pulse's quoted speech.** Pulse doesn't format; it talks.

## 6. The rewrites

### Headline

**Current (live site) — keep it.**

> # Coached, not scolded.

This is the strongest asset in the brand and it survives. It answers the reader's actual
first question (*will this make me feel like a failure again?*) in three words, and "scolded"
is a word no competitor can print — MyFitnessPal saying it would be a confession, and the
telehealth brands are in the wrong register for it. Replacing a working, ownable, three-word
line for the sake of novelty would be trading an asset for a redesign.

**Subheadline — rewritten.**

Before:

> Every other nutrition app is a scorecard of your failures. Footing is the coach who notices
> when you're winning — and tells you exactly what to do when you're not.

After:

> On a GLP-1 the goal flips: it's not about eating less, it's about eating *enough*. Footing
> tracks the protein floor that protects your results — and puts a coach in your corner
> who's actually read your whole day.

**Why it's better:** the old version spends its first sentence attacking a competitor, which
makes the brand the subject. The new one spends it handing the reader language for something
they've felt and never had named — the goal inverting. That creates immediate authority
("this person knows something I don't"), and *then* names the mechanism. Emotional-then-
specific beats specific-then-emotional. It also front-loads "GLP-1," which qualifies the
audience in the first four words.

### About paragraph

Before (§06 on the live site):

> A footing is the base poured beneath a floor so nothing above it sinks. On a GLP-1, protein
> is that base — the shot does its part, and your protein floor is what keeps the result
> standing. Most apps in this category are named after the medication. This one is named
> after the work.

After:

> A footing is the base poured beneath a floor so nothing above it sinks.
>
> On a GLP-1, protein is that base. The shot does its part — and your protein floor is what
> keeps the result standing once it has.
>
> Most apps in this category are named after the medication. This one is named after **the
> work**.

**Why it's better:** same words, three moves. The definition is pulled out alone and set in
the serif italic, so it reads as an aphorism rather than as the first half of a sentence.
"Once it has" is added to close the loop — it makes explicit that the shot's part *finishes*
and yours doesn't. And the last two sentences get their own paragraph so the stinger lands
with air around it, which is where the rhythm rule earns its keep.

### Three feature blurbs

**01 — Effortless in**

> ### Logging takes a sentence, not a search.
>
> Tell Pulse what you ate the way you'd tell a friend. It pulls real nutrition data, shows
> you the breakdown, and logs it in a tap. The tedious part — the part that made you quit
> last time — is gone.

*Unchanged from the live site except "Talk to Pulse" → "Tell Pulse." Talking to something is
a chore; telling someone is a conversation. The closing clause is the best sentence in the
original copy: it names the reader's actual history without blaming them for it.*

**02 — Honest, but useful**

> ### It tells you the truth, then what to do about it.
>
> Short on protein? Pulse won't just flag it. It'll tell you exactly how to close the gap,
> with food you actually eat. Honest when you need it — never just to make you feel small.

*Headline rewritten from "A coach who's paying attention." That was a claim about a
character; this is a claim about a behaviour, and behaviour is checkable. It also states the
consequence→path structure that the persona bible mandates, so the blurb demonstrates the
rule it describes. "Foods you actually eat" → "food you actually eat" — the mass noun is
warmer.*

**03 — Earned celebration**

> ### It notices when you win.
>
> Three days of hitting your floor. Every goal met before dinner. Pulse says so — because the
> moment you feel good about showing up is the moment you come back tomorrow. That's the part
> every other app forgot.

*Headline tightened from "It celebrates when you win" — "celebrates" oversells and edges
toward the cheerleader failure mode; "notices" is the exact promise, and it echoes the
hero's core idea. The two opening fragments replace two questions; fragments land harder than
rhetorical questions, which always read a little salesy.*

## 7. Reusable style guide

### The section formula

Every section on the page follows the same four-beat shape. Use it for new pages.

1. **Eyebrow** — two or three words, small caps, warm amber. Names the beat. *"The problem."
   "The coach." "The name."*
2. **Headline** — one sentence, under 12 words, sentence case. Makes a claim you can check.
3. **Body** — two to four sentences. Specific, numbers where they exist, one idea.
4. **The turn** — a single short line that pivots to what it means for the reader. This is
   where the gradient emphasis goes, once per section at most.

### The four-question check for any new line

1. **Could a competitor print this?** If yes, it's generic. Add a number or a specific.
2. **Does it grade the reader?** Any implication that they failed — rewrite.
3. **Would Pulse say it out loud?** Read it in Pulse's register. If it sounds like a landing
   page, it is one.
4. **Does it earn its length?** Cut the sentence that only restates the one before it.

### Before / after, for calibration

| Instead of | Write |
|---|---|
| "Unlock your weight loss journey!" | "On a GLP-1 the goal flips." |
| "Simply log your meals in seconds." | "Logging takes a sentence, not a search." |
| "AI-powered nutrition insights." | "It already read your day." |
| "Stay accountable and crush your goals." | "It notices when you win." |
| "You went over your calorie goal." | "530 calories left if you want them." |
| "Don't break your streak!" | "Three days running now. That's a first." |

### Two things that are always true

- **Any claim about health data, privacy, or what reaches a model provider must be verified
  in code before it ships.** The FAQ currently carries a deliberately unmissable
  `TODO — do not ship` block instead of an answer. That is the correct default: no claim goes
  on the page until someone has read the Supabase policies and the `coach-chat` Edge
  Function.
- **Never write a testimonial that doesn't exist.** Pre-launch, proof comes from real product
  screens, a real Pulse exchange, and a founder note that's true. The slot for real quotes is
  designed; it stays empty until TestFlight fills it.
